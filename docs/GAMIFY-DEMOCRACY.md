# Gamify Democracy: contribution and recognition

User-directed scope, 7 November 2026 (Australia/Brisbane). This is a core membership capability to build. The user's P4A banner explicitly presents **Gamify Democracy** as part of the project's identity.

The user expressly wants leaderboards for **monetary contribution** and **voluntary contribution**. The Strange But True [Community Ledger](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/strange-but-true/community-ledger.html#support-records) supplies an example of contribution recognition alongside separate voluntary C-Hours. The user clarified that iCi/SOL is their personal Strange But True project and that they have already minted a token on Solana. The page's proposed paid-contribution and leaderboard integration is distinct from that existing token. Its iCi/SOL/XRP references are source context, not a P4A asset or network direction.

P4A must choose its own precise Australian payment, asset and regulatory arrangements. Carry forward the contribution-recognition concept without importing a personal token dependency. No cryptocurrency, network or provider has been selected for P4A; this does not exclude any network from a fair assessment against P4A's requirements.

The local P4A architecture also describes contribution records across personal, crew/project, local and wider civic scales, with opt-in badges/ranks, source records, human review and challenges. This complements the user's explicit leaderboard requirement. Earlier comparison-only wording must not be used to remove the requested leaderboards.

## What the membership application must provide

| View | Recognises | Units and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monetary contribution leaderboard | People and organisations funding shared work | Verified eligible financial receipts, stated currency/valuation basis and corrections; separately identified receipt categories |
| Voluntary contribution leaderboard | Freely given human work | Independently verified C-Hours: one human hour counted once, with no conversion into money |
| Paid project contribution view | Work delivered through the paid economy | Paid-work/invoice and delivery evidence; separate from giving money and from voluntary hours |
| Project and community outcomes | What the contributions helped accomplish | Accepted milestones, source evidence, review dates and unresolved questions |

Financial support, paid work and voluntary time may appear side by side. Do not silently add wages earned, money donated and hours volunteered into a single score. Any later composite methodology requires an explicit proposal and review. Contributions must remain meaningful in their original units.

Provide views for individuals and teams, projects and local nodes, with council/electorate, state and federal summaries as the corresponding organisational/geographic records are established. Start from actual relationships and verified project locations; do not invent established party branches. Every board needs a defined period, scope, metric, eligibility rule and last-updated time. Support all-time and chosen reporting-period views alongside recent milestones and rotating recognition of useful work.

The public presentation must let people understand the work behind a position: the relevant project, contribution category, accepted receipt or appropriately redacted evidence, verification status, resulting milestone and correction/appeal route. The source's local-node pattern can connect Strange But True to other participating nodes through the same receipt schema and attribution rules.

## Contribution accounting and public choice

The monetary board should calculate contribution from the verified original financial event under its published method. The method must specify how gross receipts, fees, refunds, chargebacks, restricted funds and currency conversion affect displayed totals. A purchase of cryptocurrency or a later increase in its price does not create a second contribution from the donor. Transfers between P4A entities and project allocations must preserve provenance without counting the same original gift repeatedly at a combined level. Paid work delivered is recognised in its own category.

C-Hours require human contribution, relevant evidence and independent verification. Duplicate receipts and overlapping/impossible claims must be checked. Corrections, withdrawals and verification revocations must update the board with a reviewable history. Quality, impact, care and quiet work can be described and recognised without inventing a monetary exchange rate for time.

Different qualifying contributions receive equal time recognition. Paid shifts cannot be relabelled voluntary, and machine processing time is separate from human C-Hours. The constitution workbench must settle qualifying work, verifier authority, conflicts, fraud handling and relevant community/cultural protocols.

Named participation is opt-in. A person's optional public name, membership and ZKP-backed identity verification can accompany their contribution profile, subject to their exact disclosure choices. Financial and voluntary leaderboard publication need their own clear choices and preview. The existing `publicConsent` on contribution receipts authorises **aggregate publication only** and must not be reused as consent for named ranks. Required statutory disclosures are handled according to their own legal basis and explained separately.

An undisclosed individual's contribution can support an authorised aggregate without exposing their identity or assigning a named public rank. Avoid re-identification through small groups, activity descriptions, timing and wallet links. Withdrawal of optional disclosure removes future display while retaining only records needed under the actual retention/legal rules.

Ranking methodology and disputes need independent human oversight. Contribution recognition is not a statement of someone's overall worth, proof of honesty or an automatic allocation of political authority. The constitution workbench must provide a place to settle the relationship between recognition, responsibilities and decision rights. Existing verified-hour rules grant no additional political entitlement.

## Blockchain and payment integration

The payment/blockchain design must be evaluated for the broader contribution ecosystem as well as network-fee funding. Candidate benefits include portable contribution receipts, attributable project funding, independently verifiable ledger checkpoints, privacy-preserving proofs of selected totals and cross-node recognition without repeated counting.

Link, with appropriate permissions:

**Verified identity / membership → accepted contribution receipt → project or node allocation → consented recognition → evidence of outcomes.**

Identity and membership credentials, monetary receipts, voluntary receipts and outcome attestations are separate claims with appropriate issuers and review/status rules. A ZKP can prove a defined statement over valid credentials or committed records. It cannot establish that every real-world contribution has been recorded or that an unverified outcome is true. Regulators and auditors still need their required underlying records.

An optional token conversion can fund an approved function or support a specifically reviewed contribution mechanism. Buying a token must have a documented purpose and accounting treatment. Receipt issuance and public recognition need not require each contributor to buy a token. Strange But True's personal token is not a default candidate, required asset or entitlement in P4A. A cross-chain design needs explicit interoperability, cost, custody, privacy and governance review.

Do not publish raw membership lists, identity documents or private donor/volunteer records on-chain. Use privacy-reviewed commitments and member-authorised presentations, with status freshness, expiry, revocation and correction. Public ledger entries must not expose a person's wallet activity unintentionally.

### Australian selection criteria

The following is a proposed design direction, checked against primary guidance on 7 November 2026. It does not select an asset or establish legal clearance for a live service.

- Use AUD as the primary reporting and monetary-leaderboard unit, retaining original currency/quantity, valuation source and time. Keep ordinary AUD payments available. Independently assess accepting crypto gifts, converting P4A's own settled funds and providing services to members.
- Evaluate each asset's rights, intended use and related services under Australian law. ASIC assesses the overall arrangement; a ticker, Australian branding or overseas classification does not settle it. AUD stablecoins can be financial products and require review of applicable licensing or relief. [ASIC digital-asset guidance](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/digital-transformation/digital-assets-financial-products-and-services)
- Verify the provider's current AUSTRAC registration for the applicable virtual-asset service and the relevant ASIC authorisations or exemptions. Treat registration as a service-provider check, not token endorsement. [AUSTRAC provider guidance](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/your-industry/virtual-asset-service-providers/virtual-asset-service-providers-overview)
- Compare an AUD-denominated settlement asset, a native network-fee asset and fiat-billed blockchain infrastructure by their actual functions. Check redemption rights, reserves, custody, liquidity, total cost, reliability, security, ZKP compatibility and privacy. A stablecoin and a network's fee token need not be the same asset. No requirement to mint a P4A tradable token follows from membership or contribution recognition.
- Map donor eligibility, receipt classification, disclosure, permitted spending and required accounts separately for each actual entity and jurisdiction. For entities subject to the federal-account regime commencing 1 January 2027, the account must be with an Australian ADI and kept in Australia; a token wallet does not meet that definition. [AEC reform guidance](https://www.aec.gov.au/FADReform/faqs.htm)

Use a dated comparison with evidence for each candidate before choosing the payment/chain combination. Distinguish enacted rules and commencement dates from proposals. Australian member preferences for familiarity, payment methods and wallet use should be tested with members rather than inferred from a blockchain's popularity.

## Implementation and acceptance

Current local build: the Gamify Democracy route provides separate named monetary and voluntary leaderboards, per-receipt publication choices, private AUD financial records, independent review, reasoned corrections, recorded refund/chargeback adjustments and verification revocation. Existing records gain no named consent automatically. All-time and current Brisbane calendar-year views can be filtered by eligible working group and public project. Demo records are fictional.

This component is a manually reviewed contribution subledger, not live payment settlement, a general ledger or a regulator filing service. A verified financial receipt records an organiser's independent evidence/settlement confirmation. It does not establish KYC, statutory donor eligibility or provider reconciliation. Public member identity profiles, paid-work accounting, cross-node credentials, ZKP and blockchain integration remain outstanding. No crypto asset or network is selected; no token is purchased. Do not fill boards with purported real contributors or infer consent from public source links.

The home page and navigation link to Gamify Democracy. Private member exports include the member's own financial receipts and histories; public views use a separate projection. Monetary totals count original verified AUD receipts less recorded refunds/chargebacks, without deducting transaction fees. Adjustments restate the original receipt period. Named rows use only separately consented receipts; public totals include the union of aggregate-consented and named-consented receipts. Equal totals share a competition rank.

A substantive correction returns a receipt to pending review, clears publication choices and keeps its prior version in history. Verification withdrawal removes it from the boards. Sharing withdrawal changes future publication independently of the receipt's validity. Organisers cannot verify their own contributions or financial receipts they entered for someone else.

Operational disputes, paid-work records, cross-node deduplication, organisation-specific finance and regulatory filing remain development work. Monetary integration depends on the actual payment, entity and reporting design. Review fixtures must be explicitly fictional.

Acceptance checks must demonstrate:

- Only qualifying verified records enter the applicable board; unpublished identities and evidence do not leak through the UI, API or exports.
- Replayed payment events, mirrored receipts across nodes and internal transfers cannot inflate totals.
- Refunds, chargebacks, corrected hours and revoked verification update totals consistently while preserving audit history.
- Ranking order, tied positions, reporting periods and currencies follow the published methodology reproducibly.
- Declining or withdrawing named recognition works independently of private membership, KYC and valid contribution records.
- Individual/project/node totals reconcile with the underlying scoped ledger, without turning token-market gains into donor contributions.
- Any credential/proof badge checks actual issuer authority, binding, validity and current status, as specified in [Identity and transparency](IDENTITY-AND-TRANSPARENCY.md).
