# Constitution drafting workbench

The workbench supports collaborative constitutional drafting for P4A the Purple Party for Australia. P4A currently has no adopted constitution and is not a registered political party. These records are proposals for discussion, with no adoption or registration implied by their presence in the application.

## Source and scope

The ten starter clauses in [constitution-seed.mjs](../src/constitution-seed.mjs) are newly written discussion wording based on Luke's membership brief and the existing [public constitution workbench](https://p4a.xyz/pages/constitution.html). That page provides design guidance about participation, human authority, transparency, private records, local rule suites and constitutional evolution. It does not supply an enacted constitution. The starter wording is not copied adopted text, a statement of current binding rules or a completed legal instrument.

Joyful Responsible Abundance supplies the shared purpose. The clauses connect grassroots participation and local autonomy with the possibility of separately governed state and federal organisations. Local charters, movement rules and later party constitutions must identify their own scope, affected people, decision authority and adoption process. One organisation's draft must not silently become another's governing instrument.

The starter wording deliberately leaves admission criteria, fees, voting eligibility, quorums, delegation limits, office powers, selection procedures, disciplinary timeframes and adoption thresholds for participants to settle. It does not invent those choices or treat a software role as a constitutional office. Paid services, working-group participation and political rights remain separate.

## Collaborative drafting and human decisions

A useful drafting record preserves the clause, version, proposed replacement, proposer, reason, evidence and discussion. Review should make the change understandable and show the previous wording. A proposal based on an older clause version needs reconsideration against the current text rather than silently overwriting newer work.

An authorised drafting reviewer may merge an agreed edit into the working draft. That action records editorial progress; it does not enact the text, register a party or demonstrate that members have adopted it. Proposal closure or revision should retain the reason and history so participants can follow what happened.

Formal adoption requires a separately established, human-led process appropriate to the relevant organisation. That work must settle participation and decision rules, check applicable requirements, resolve outstanding provisions and record the actual decision and adopted version. The drafting software must not label a merge, organiser approval or display of a clause as constitutional adoption.

Keep public drafting material and personal information distinct. Source links and an explanation of a public issue can support discussion without copying private household, legal, health or Aura records into the workbench. AI assistance may help compare and improve wording; accountable people decide the rules.

## Implemented access and future governance

All authenticated participants in this workspace can read its working drafts, propose clause replacements and discuss amendments. Organisers create documents and review proposals, with self-review rejected. Document scope keeps different drafts and version histories distinct; it is not yet a separate party permission boundary. Future party workspaces need their own verified authority, entity access and admission arrangements before confidential party records are introduced.

The current editor replaces existing clause text and proposes new clauses through the same discussion and independent review process. Deleting, renaming or rearranging clauses, formal ballots, delegation and adoption records remain further development. The public P4A design workbench stays open; internal discussion is not automatically published. Draft exports are clearly marked as unadopted.
